What to do

  • Get to 20 every day. A live day brings the players and makes the next day easier.
  • Start earlier. Hours gained before 11pm are hours the server keeps.
  • Put every seeder on one server first. Let the second fill from it.
  • Leave map rotation and team balance alone. Neither moves the numbers.

What to try, in order

  • Seed as a squad of five at a set time, with at least one admin in it.
  • Call for 10, not 20.
  • Do not quit early. If still under 5 after an hour and a half, send a fresh call.
  • Move the seed to late morning.
  • Add a Seeder role with an automatic reward, and ask the regulars who have never seeded.

Redline rises and falls with the game and with other servers.

Keep the streak going, and have admins playing.

What this page cannot see

  • In-game chat. It is not part of the data this page reads.
  • What the reviews say. Only the counts are read.
  • The Redline queue. Neither BattleMetrics nor WarCon reports it.
  • Discord after November 17, 2026. Those figures come from a one-time export, and only message counts were used.

What this means

  • Protect the streak. A dark day makes the next day harder, so a short seeded night is worth more than it looks.
  • Judge Redline against the game. Watch players per 10,000 in the game, not the raw count.
  • Do not rely on Discord posts to seed. People in the server do it.
  • Expect dips you did not cause. Seasons fade after a week, and outside releases pull players away.

How these numbers are worked out

Built every Monday from BattleMetrics, WarCon and Steam; not a live feed. Typical means the middle value. Player counts are rebuilt minute by minute from BattleMetrics join and leave records; a session with no recorded end, or longer than 4 hours, is counted as 40 minutes. A seed attempt is a stretch where a server goes from empty to at least 3 players. A new player is the first ever join on that server. A match change is the end of one match and the start of the next with 60 or more players on, from WarCon; lost a third means two thirds or fewer were left 30 minutes later. Mode figures cover community servers that had 3 or more players on at some point in the week. Season dates are the official posts on Steam. Players in the game is the daily high from steamcharts.com. Links are Spearman rank correlations over days. The Where we rank page is read from WARDOGS Server List, which checks every server about every 5 minutes; its ranks are by average players on across the week, with hours offline counted as zero. Counts that depend on a 10-minute cut-off, such as how many players seeded once, can move by a few either way. The scripts are in the growth folder of the repository.
